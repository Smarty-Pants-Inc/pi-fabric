Original investigation premise was lead-supplied `spawnSync(flock -w 10)` with approximately **11 s child timeout**, not an independently inspected app. Parent later verified and inspected the full application archive:

The independent R2 challenger identified **3–4 waits in a single add path**, which the R1 single-11-second model undercounted:

Each wait can approach 10 s before acquiring (or hit child timeout); successful sequential waits can make **one call stall the resident for 30 s or more**. A failing first wait may terminate that call earlier; this does not remove the successful multi-lock contention counterexample. A one-off 11 s synthetic stall is useful negative evidence, not the worst-case actual handler proof. List/resolve and their reachable write/read/refusal-audit paths must also remain safe when used by the real RPC canary. No conversion solely of `withStoreLock` qualifies.

The owning no-model Pi **Main is another process**. Blocking the native **resident** does not block Main heartbeat, but a healthy Main cannot renew the resident's lease or confirm its consumer freshness.

## Default budgets and effect-time gates

Slack for an 11 s block with measured pre-stall age `a`: freshness slack = `10 − a − 11` seconds; resident lease slack = `15 − a − 11`. Near the normal next renewal, `a ≈ 5`, so return is near age 16 s: ~6 s beyond freshness and ~1 s beyond lease. These are nominal steady-state calculations, not scheduler guarantees; arbitrarily delayed initial state has no positive guaranteed slack. A ≥30 s block crosses both by much more.
